Machine bricks are all the same. Yours should not be.

Extruded bricks come off a line with sharp, dead-straight edges and a flat, uniform face. On a restoration in a Mallorcan finca or a village townhouse, that reads as wrong the moment the light moves across the wall. A hand-moulded brick has soft edges, a folded, sandy face and a colour that shifts brick to brick — so a new wall settles into an old one instead of fighting it.

How they are made

Clay, hands, air, fire.

  1. 01

    Preparing the clay

    Local clay is broken down, cleaned of stones and roots, then mixed with water and sand until it takes a mould cleanly and holds its shape.

  2. 02

    Throwing the mould

    The timber mould is dusted with sand, a clot of clay is thrown in hard enough to reach the corners, and the surplus is struck off level by hand.

  3. 03

    Drying

    Green bricks are turned out onto boards and dried slowly under cover. Rushing this stage is what cracks a brick, so it takes as long as the weather says it takes.

  4. 04

    Firing

    Once bone dry, the bricks are fired. Position in the kiln changes the colour, and that is where the variation across a pallet comes from.
